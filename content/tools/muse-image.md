Muse Image, launched July 7 by Meta Superintelligence Labs, is Meta's first entirely in-house image model — and it replaces the third-party engines (Midjourney, Black Forest Labs) that previously powered image generation inside Meta AI. The architecture is different from most generators: it's an "agent model" that searches the web for context, writes its own prompts, and refines its own results. Ask it about a current event and it doesn't hallucinate the details — it looks them up first. It can render precise in-image text, generate accurate charts, and even validates a QR code before embedding it, so the output actually scans.

The honesty caveats matter here. Meta reports Muse Image ranks #2 on its text-to-image arena (Elo 1,280, behind GPT Image 2) — but these are vendor-reported, unaudited figures. And the launch was not spotless: the ability to @-mention public Instagram accounts as image references was removed just three days after launch, after a privacy backlash from SAG-AFTRA and creators. That's the kind of detail most directory listings skip, and it's the kind of thing we flag.

The access model is the real limitation. Muse Image has no public API, no open weights, and no developer model card — it exists only inside Meta's apps. That's fine if you live in Instagram and WhatsApp, and the free tier with usage caps is generous. But if you want to build on it, or generate images outside Meta's ecosystem, it's not an option — Midjourney or the API-first providers remain the choice.
