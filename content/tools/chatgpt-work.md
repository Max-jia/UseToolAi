ChatGPT Work is OpenAI's answer to Claude Cowork, and it launched with unusual honesty in its own materials: it's "designed for tasks that are longer and more complex than normal conversations" and "may consume more of your plan's included usage." In plain terms — this is not a flat-rate feature. Work draws down the same agent-credit system as Codex, and long sessions eat into your monthly allowance. The July 31 price cuts on the Luna and Terra models (80% and 20% cheaper) soften that, but the metering is the real cost story, not the subscription price.

The product itself is the most complete consumer-facing office agent to date. Plan mode is the standout design choice: the agent gathers context from your connected apps, asks clarifying questions, then presents a plan you can revise before it executes. That approval gate is why this works for normal businesses — you're not handing an autonomous agent the keys, you're reviewing its itinerary first. The 1,400+ plugin integrations and cross-device cloud sync make it feel like the deliverable-producing agent that Cowork and Copilot have been racing toward.

The honest caveats: rollout has been phased (Pro and Enterprise first, then Plus and Business), the deepest workflows are desktop-only, and enterprise pricing is opaque. If you're on an existing ChatGPT plan, it's worth trying this week — it costs nothing extra to test. If you're choosing between agents for a team, the credit metering and plan tiers matter more than the demo videos. Pricing checked August 10, 2026.
